# Pre-specification of gate G-F2.1

**The stamp** is the last commit on the repository's branch that changes `docs/G-F2.1_code.sha256`; it also adds this document. When that commit was made, no member of the family had been drawn with the seed of the sample ("The sample"), and neither of the gate's two training configurations had been run with the seeds 5, 6 or 7 on any mesh.

## Question

The development of 20 to 25 November 2026 (`docs/F2_development_note.md`) arrived at a label-free surrogate of the bolted CHS flange-plate splices of `docs/F2_preparation_note.md`: a network that is trained on the total potential energy of the reference model alone, with no reference solution, and that gives the moment-rotation curve of a splice it has not seen. Everything the development knows about its accuracy comes from one training run, with seed 0, read on 31 members that were watched while the recipe was chosen.

G-F2.1 asks whether that accuracy holds for training runs and members that the development has not seen.

**Hypothesis.** Trained three times by the recipe fixed below, with three seeds with which neither of its two training configurations has been run, and read after the amplitude condensation on 64 members of the family that are drawn after the stamp, the surrogate gives the reference model's moment at the same rotation of the measuring ring within 5% in the median and within 10% at the 90th percentile, each as the median over the three runs.

**From the development, unchanged** (`docs/F2_preparation_note.md` and `docs/F2_development_note.md`, which this document does not repeat):
- the reference model, its theory (J2 deformation theory, small strain, unilateral contact, a frictionless splice plane), its mesh (the family's mesh, called coarse in the code) and its solver settings;
- the family: five parameters drawn uniformly and independently, the plate thickness (10 to 25 mm), the two edge distances of the bolts (55 to 75 mm each), the plate's yield stress (345 to 460 MPa) and the bolt preload (60 to 130 kN);
- the loading through a rotational spring, the device range of each member, and the seven levels at 0.0625, 0.125, 0.25, 0.375, 0.5, 0.75 and 1 of that range;
- the network, the label-free loss and the two training cycles of the recipe, with every setting;
- the raw and the condensed reading;
- the 128 training members (seed 20261120) and the 32 further training members with thin plates (seed 20261124). They are exported with their preload state and never solved further.

**New in the gate**
1. **The training seeds** are 5, 6 and 7. The development trained with seed 0 on the family's mesh and with seeds 0, 1 and 2 on a smaller mesh; the seeds 90 to 93 are kept for timings of two epochs each ("Host and budget"). The gate's command trains with no seed but its three.
2. **The sample.** 64 members drawn after the stamp are judged. The 31 members of the development are reported and not judged.
3. **Three runs instead of one**, each trained from new networks through both cycles.

## Where the recipe comes from

The second cycle of the recipe is not the one that the development's own rule prescribed, and this section says so plainly.

- On 22 November 2026 the first family run on the family's mesh (the first cycle below, seed 0) was followed by a second cycle from its final weights, at a lower learning rate. On the 31 members of the development the error of the moment at the same rotation of the measuring ring, read after the condensation, was 0.99% in the median and 3.69% at the 90th percentile, with a largest error of 56.9% in the thinnest hand-picked member at its last level.
- On 24 November 2026 two changes of that second cycle were tried, each once, under a rule written before the runs (`probes/f2_stage2/PRESPEC.md`): the device rotations of the training steps drawn uniformly and up to 1.15 times the member's device range (arm 1), and the same with 32 further training members with plates of 10 to 12.5 mm (arm 2). An arm passed if six measures on the 31 members all held. **Neither arm passed.** Both met five of the six and missed the same one: the median error at the two lowest levels, 0.79% in the control, had to stay at or below 0.87% and was 1.04% and 1.01%. By the rule the second cycle of 22 November 2026 was to be kept.
- **The gate uses arm 2 all the same**, by the owner's decision of 25 November 2026 on the author's recommendation. The reasons given to the owner: the trial was exploratory and served only to choose the recipe; arm 2 lowered the largest error from 56.9% to 12.2%, the 90th percentile from 3.69% to 2.99% and the median error at the last level in thin plates from 7.07% to 2.78%, against 0.2 percentage points more at the two lowest levels; and the line it missed had been set by the author at one tenth above a small number. The author also told the owner that the last reason was found after the result was known, that the rule had been written to guard against exactly such a reading, and that keeping the rule would be fully proper.
- **What arm 2 cost**, in full (`records/f2_dev/family_mesh/trial_24_november/outside_the_rule.txt`): at the two lowest levels the median error rose from 0.79% to 1.01% over all members and from 1.97% to 2.63% in the eight members with a plate thinner than 12.5 mm, where the gate expects its largest errors; 27 of the 31 members got worse at those two levels; the median over all pairs rose from 0.99% to 1.07%, and over the eleven hand-picked members from 1.54% to 1.82%.
- **A second sentence of that rule was not kept either.** The plan of 24 November 2026 ended: "Whatever the outcome, no further change is tried before the gate." On 25 November 2026, on the author's proposal and the owner's word (the message of 02:03 above), a two-level recipe (a correction to the field of a network trained on a smaller mesh) was tried once under a rule fixed before the run (`probes/f2_under/PRESPEC.md`), and the gate waited for it. It fell into its rule's lowest class, 4.69% in the median and 11.64% at the 90th percentile on the 31 members, and is not part of the recipe: the recipe is as the trial of 24 November 2026 left it.
- What this means for the gate: the recipe was chosen after its results on the 31 members were seen, against a rule that said otherwise. The gate's verdict does not rest on those members. It rests on members drawn after the stamp and on training runs with seeds that the recipe's configurations have not been run with. Whether the choice was a good one shows in whether the gate passes.

## Expected outcome, stated before the run

The author expects a PASS, with a median near 1% and a 90th percentile near 3%, and puts the chance of a PASS at about six in seven.
- The lines are far from the one run seen: 5% and 10% against 1.07% and 2.99%. On the smaller mesh three seeds differed by about a fifth around their median. A sample drawn at random holds fewer extreme members than the 31, which include eleven picked at corners.
- The main risk is not the accuracy of a run that trains but a run that does not: a non-finite loss fails the gate, and a run that finishes without having trained enters with its errors. None of the five runs in the table above stopped or failed to train, but only one seed was trained on the family's mesh. In the T-stub family the first gate, before the stability settings that this recipe uses, lost one seed of three: its loss rose early and stayed (`docs/G-C2.1_verdict.md`). The median over three seeds tolerates one such run; it does not tolerate a non-finite stop.
- Members with thin plates will carry the largest errors. About one sixth of a sample has a plate of at most 12.5 mm.

Because the lines are loose at this level, a PASS says "within 5% and 10%" and no more. The numbers that a paper may quote are the reported ones.

## The sample

**How it is drawn.** 64 members, uniformly and independently over the family's ranges, by the function that drew the development's 16 random members (`f2.family.sample`); a draw whose bearing rings would reach the fillet's toe or the plate's edge is drawn again.

**Its seed** is `20261125`: 25 November 2026, the day on which the stamp was prepared, written as the integer YYYYMMDD. It is none of the seeds that drew members of the family before the stamp, which are 11, 12, 20260102, 20260103, 20261120, 20261121, 20261124 and 20261127 (`f21/config.py`).

**A rule that was dropped before the stamp.** As first written, the seed was to be the day of the stamp's commit in UTC, and a stamp whose commit carried another day was to be void and made again with that day. The docstring of `f21/stamp_day.py` and the `seed_rule` text of the configuration still say so. On 25 November 2026, before the stamp, the owner decided that the seed stays as it is whatever day the commit carries ("Authority"). A commit on a later day therefore does not make the stamp void, and the two texts in the code are read with this paragraph; they were left as they were because the tests, the host check and the simulation had run on those files. The dropped rule would have let anyone check the seed against the repository's history alone. What remains is this: the seed is in the hashed configuration and in the stamped code, which the stamp's commit adds; it is the date that this document, the records of `records/G-F2.1/before_stamp/` and the independent readings carry; and nothing was drawn with it before the stamp ("What was seen before the stamp"). The verdict document gives the day of the stamp's commit beside the seed.

**How the code holds to it.** The seed is written into `f21/stamp_day.py` when the stamp is prepared; it is part of the hashed configuration and of the stamped code. Every command refuses to run under another configuration hash, so that no command can draw the sample with another seed, in any folder. The command that exports the sample also refuses a seed that is not a day from 25 November 2026 on, a used seed and a day that has not come. It writes the seed, the configuration hash and a hash of the drawn parameters to `sample.json` before it exports the first member, and every later command refuses a sample folder whose record is not this configuration's. The functions configure the gate's sample only while a command of the gate runs, after that command has found the code to be the stamped code; at any other time they refuse, so that a test, a check or a look at the code does not draw it by a call. This guards against a slip and not against intent: it is a flag in the code, which a caller could set, as a caller could write a stamp file.

**What the code cannot check** is that nothing was drawn with the seed before the stamp. That rests on the author's record ("What was seen before the stamp") and on the guard described above; the repository's history shows the seed in the stamp's commit and in no earlier one.

**When it is drawn.** After the stamp and before the trainings. The training code is never given the sample's folder.

**When it is spent.** Once its first member has been exported, whatever happens afterwards. No later gate is judged on these 64 members, and anything computed on them after this gate is exploratory and is reported as such.

## Model and training

**Network** (`f2/model.py`, `f2/devconfig.py`): the repository's transformer encoder over the nodes of the mesh (width 256, 8 layers, 8 heads), a copy of its field decoder, and a head that gives the unknowns that belong to no node. The features of a node are its coordinates, its boundary and part flags, the five family parameters and the fraction of the member's device range. The tube's displacements are given as a correction to the motion of a beam in pure bending that follows the end rotation (the lift called bending). The contact unknowns pass through a softplus.

**Loss.** For a member and a device rotation, the network's field has a total potential energy Π in the reference model with the loading spring; the loss of a step is the mean of log Π over four device rotations of one member. No reference solution enters training.

**The first cycle** (`fam128_bending_e400`): new networks, with the seed set before they are built. 400 epochs of one step on each of the 128 training members in a fresh random order, 51,200 steps. AdamW with a peak learning rate of 1e-3, a weight decay of 1e-4 and a second-moment coefficient of 0.99; a linear warm-up over the first 5% of the steps and a cosine schedule after it; the cap on the encoder's gradient at five times its running median and the global clipping at 1.0, as in gate G-C2.2. The device rotations of a step are drawn with the square root of the rotation uniform between 0.0125 and 1 of the member's device range. Weights, activations and energy in single precision.

**The second cycle** (`fam128_bending_more_ux`): from the final weights of the same seed's first cycle, with a new optimiser and a new schedule. 160 epochs of one step on each of 160 training members, the 128 and the 32 further ones, 25,600 steps. A peak learning rate of 3e-4. The device rotations are drawn uniformly between 0.0125 and 1.15 of the member's device range, so that some lie beyond the last evaluation level. Everything else as in the first cycle. The command refuses to start a second cycle whose first cycle is not finished.

**Seeds.** 5, 6 and 7, each for both cycles of its run.

**The end of a cycle.** After its last step the training command predicts the 31 members of the development, as in the development. A cycle is finished when its record says so and these predictions are written; a cycle that was cut off between the two goes on at the predictions when its command is run again, and until then nothing of the gate treats it as finished.

**Predictions on the sample.** No seed predicts the sample before every training of the gate has ended: the command refuses while a cycle of any seed is still to run. Then each seed's final state predicts every member of the sample at the seven levels, in both readings. Before that the command checks the chain of hashes: the two run records are this seed's finished runs of the two pinned configurations, the second cycle started from the first cycle's final state, and the state file is the end of this seed's second cycle. Its record names the two states and the prediction files by their SHA-256. The readings of the predictions are compared with the sample's labels by the evaluation and by nothing else.

**The evaluation of the sample** is made once, when every seed has predictions on the sample or, after a stop for good, a record that it has none. It runs the checks of the labels first and refuses if they fail, refuses prediction files other than those the predictions' records name, and writes with its results the SHA-256 of every file it read. The verdict refuses an evaluation whose inputs are not those on record.

**The two readings** (`c22/condense.py`, as in gate G-C2.2). Raw: the network's field at the level's device rotation. Condensed: the field keep(z0 + a (z - z0)), with z0 the network's field at 0.0125 of the device range, keep setting the contact unknowns to at least zero, and the amplitude a minimising the training energy over 0.5 to 3 with at most 12 energy evaluations. No reference solution enters either reading. The condensed reading is the judged one.

## The judged quantity

For a member and a level, the level's device rotation is Θ, and the field read from the network gives the end rotation θ, the moment M = 2 k (Θ - θ) through the loading spring of stiffness k per half model, and the rotation of the measuring ring Θ_D, the rotation that the tests report.

The error of the pair is e = |M / M*(Θ_D) - 1|, with M*(Θ_D) the moment of the reference model at the same rotation of the measuring ring. It is computed as in the development (`f2/evaluate.py`, `curveD_err`): one exact solve under rotation control at the predicted end rotation gives a point (Θ_D', M') of the reference curve, and M*(Θ_D) is taken as M' + s (Θ_D - Θ_D'), with s the slope at Θ_D' of a monotone cubic through the member's labelled path. The cubic is scipy's piecewise cubic Hermite interpolant (`PchipInterpolator`) through the origin, which stands for the preload state, and the seven labelled points (Θ_D, M); beyond the last point it is continued by its last piece. This is exact to first order in Θ_D - Θ_D'. The error depends on the predicted field through three numbers only, θ, M and Θ_D, which the evaluation records for every pair, so that anyone can compute it again from the returned records and the labelled paths (`probes/f21_first_order/first_order.py`).

**Infinite errors.** The error of a pair is infinite if the solve at the predicted end rotation does not converge or gives a moment that is not positive, or if the first-order reference moment is not positive. If the reference code cannot take a seed's predicted fields of a member at all (a fixed unknown that is not zero, a contact unknown that is negative, or any other error while the member's levels are read), all seven pairs of that member are infinite for that seed and reading. If only the energy of a predicted field cannot be evaluated, the pair keeps its judged value, and its energy gap and the reported quantities computed with it have no value; a statistic counts a missing value as infinite.

**The exact value, reported.** For the judged reading the reference state at the predicted Θ_D itself is also solved for (`ExportedProblem.solve_at_ThetaD`, a secant search on rotation control with at most 12 solves), and the error against its moment is reported with the same statistics (`curveD_exact_err`). It is not judged: every number of the development is the first-order value, and the lines were set against those. If a statistic of the exact value lies on the other side of its line than the judged one, the verdict stands as computed and the verdict document says so in its first paragraph.

## Criteria

For each seed: the median and the 90th percentile of e over the 448 pairs of the sample, in the condensed reading. The percentiles are numpy's default, by linear interpolation; an infinite value that enters the interpolation with a positive weight makes the percentile infinite. With 448 pairs the 90th percentile is infinite from 45 infinite pairs on and the median from 224.

**C1.** The median over the three seeds of the per-seed median is at most 0.05.

**C2.** The median over the three seeds of the per-seed 90th percentile is at most 0.10.

**Verdict.** PASS if and only if C1 and C2 hold and each of the three seeds has finished both cycles and has finite predictions on the sample. FAIL if C1 or C2 does not hold, or if a cycle of a seed has stopped for good (a non-finite loss, or predictions of the development's members at the end of the cycle that are not finite), or if the predictions of a seed on the sample are not finite. INCOMPLETE while a training, a prediction, the evaluation of the sample or the evaluation of the development's members is missing and nothing has failed. The values are compared without rounding.

## Reported, not judged

- For each seed: the median, the 90th percentile and the largest error, the number of pairs with an infinite error, and those pairs by member and level.
- The members with a plate of at most 12.5 mm on their own, for each seed.
- Each of the seven levels, for each seed.
- The five members with the largest error, for each seed.
- The raw reading: its median, 90th percentile, largest error and number of pairs, for each seed.
- For both readings, with the same four numbers, each over absolute values: the distance from the reference curve at the predicted end rotation, the error of the moment and of Θ_D at the same device rotation, and the energy gap.
- The exact value for the judged reading ("The judged quantity"), and the differences between it and the judged value.
- The 31 members of the development, for each seed, in both readings. These were watched in the development and say nothing about members not seen.
- Of each run: the training time (the sum of its epochs' seconds), the actions of the cap on the encoder's gradient, the resumes as far as the run's record holds them, and the peak memory of the process that ended the run; the sessions, with their GPUs, from the queue's record.

## Interpretation, fixed in advance

**PASS.** On 64 splices of the family drawn after the stamp, the surrogate, trained three times without any reference solution, gave the reference model's moment at the same rotation of the measuring ring within the reported median and 90th percentile. Any wording gives the reported values and not the lines; gives each seed's own values, and if a seed's own median or 90th percentile lies above its line, says that one run of three missed the line and that the gate passed by the median over the runs; says that the comparison is with the finite element model and not with tests; names the family and its mesh; says that the points are read after the amplitude condensation and gives the raw values beside them; gives the largest error, the member it occurred in and the values for thin plates; says how many pairs had an infinite error, if any, and in which members; and says that the training members and the recipe are those of the development and that the recipe was chosen on 31 members seen. If the exact value falls on the other side of a line, the wording says so.

**FAIL by C1 or C2.** The recipe did not carry to members and seeds not seen. The failing criterion, each seed's values and the worst members are given. Nothing is trained again under this gate.

**FAIL by a run that stopped for good or by predictions that are not finite.** The wording says that one training run of three failed and how. If other seeds finished both cycles, their values on the sample are given as reported, and they do not make a pass. If the stop came before any seed had finished both cycles, no number on the sample exists, because no cycle is started after such a stop ("Stop conditions"), and the wording says that.

**INCOMPLETE** is not read. If the owner ends the gate while it is INCOMPLETE, it is reported as not completed, with the reason and with whether predictions on the sample existed at that time.

**In every case** the sample is spent ("The sample").

## What the gate does not show

- Nothing about laboratory tests. Every error is against the reference model. Gate G-F1, which compared that model's two splice bounds with the tests, failed one of its three criteria (`docs/G-F1_verdict.md`); the frictionless model used here is below the measured moments of B-13 and B-19 by 3% to 8% (`docs/F2_preparation_note.md`).
- Nothing outside the family and its ranges, on another mesh, under another loading than bending raised once to the top of the member's device range (a rotation of the measuring ring of about 0.011 rad), or under another theory than deformation theory.
- Nothing about other recipes, budgets or networks, and nothing about fewer or more training members. It does not show that the recipe is a good choice among those tried: that choice was made on members seen.
- Nothing about the cost of the surrogate against the cost of the reference solve.
- Nothing about a member's error without a reference solution: no self-check is tested here.
- The training members are those of the development. The gate does not test a new draw of them.

## Stop conditions

- **The host check failing on the gate's host** after the stamp: nothing of the sample is drawn. The failure and what is changed are reported as a change after the stamp, and the code is stamped again, with the same seed, of which nothing has been drawn, before any command of the gate runs.
- **A host whose reference solves would not use PARDISO**: the commands that solve refuse it. Nothing is exported, solved or evaluated with another linear solver.
- **A preload that does not converge or is not on target**, of any member (training, development or sample): the export ends with an error, and the gate stops before any training and stays INCOMPLETE.
- **A label that does not converge or fails its checks**, of a member of the sample or of the development: the gate stops before any training and stays INCOMPLETE. The checks are the development's and, for each labelled member, that the rotation of the measuring ring rises along the labelled path and that the reference code evaluates each labelled state, which the evaluation needs. An export or a label that ends with the same error each time it is run counts as a label that fails. No other solver settings are tried. The evaluations run the checks again and refuse if they fail.
- **A non-finite loss, or predictions at the end of a cycle that are not finite**, end a run for good, and the verdict is FAIL. The run is not repeated, with its seed or with another. No cycle that has not been started is started afterwards; the command refuses it. Cycles that were under way at the same time are trained to their end, in a later session if a session's limit paused them; one of them that stops for good in its turn is reported like the first and left out of what follows. After the owner's answer the closing steps of the runbook put on record what the finished seeds gave: each seed without two finished cycles gets a record that it has no predictions on the sample, the others predict it, the evaluations are made for them, and the final verdict is written.
- **Predictions on the sample that are not finite** are final for their seed: the verdict is FAIL and the seed is not predicted again. The closing steps follow as above.
- **A checkpoint or a state file that cannot be read**: the cycle's folder is moved aside and kept, the cycle is started again from its beginning with its seed (a second cycle from the first cycle's final state; a first cycle with its second, if that existed), and the restart is reported. After a run of the gate has stopped for good no cycle is started again in this way either: a cycle whose folder was moved aside counts as not started, the command refuses it, and its seed gets the record that it has no predictions on the sample.
- **After any of these stops** nothing is run before the stop is reported and the owner has answered. The people who run the sessions keep this rule; the queue does not. A session started all the same skips the steps that are complete and goes on under the code's own refusals ("Configuration"): a step that stopped for good ends with the same error again, and after predictions on the sample that were not finite the steps that are left are the closing steps for that case. No number can change by such a session; it is reported as a difference from the runbook.
- **A session of the host that ends** (its time limit, a disconnection, compute units used up) is no stop of the gate. The interrupted command is run again in the next session and resumes from its checkpoint. A resumed run draws the same members and rotations as an uninterrupted one; that it ends in the same state to the last bit was checked on a processor in the development's tests and is not claimed for the runs of this gate. The queue's record of the sessions is the account of starts and stops; a run's own record lists a resume only if a checkpoint was written after it.
- **Library versions that differ between sessions** of the host stop the task until the author has looked. If the difference is in the host's image and cannot be undone, the task goes on under the new versions, and the change is reported with the sessions it separates.

## Host and budget

**Host.** The compute of the development: a hosted notebook runtime with one GPU, on which a queue runs the commands of the runbook one after another and copies the records to a shared folder. The library versions are recorded at the start of every session, and the task's first command prints those of numpy, scipy, pypardiso, mkl and torch. In the development's runs they were Python 3.13.16, torch 2.14.1 with CUDA 13.0, numpy 2.4.6 and scipy 1.16.3, on a runtime with 48 processors, of which the queue gave 24 to worker processes. The task file of those runs installed pypardiso 0.4.7 and mkl 2026.2.1 by version, and the gate's task does the same; the sessions' records of the development do not hold these two versions, only the label ledger's entry that every label was solved with PARDISO.

**The GPU.** A task accepts GPU models out of three: the RTX PRO 6000 of the development's runs, the H100 and the A100; a runtime with another GPU is released at once. Which of the accepted models a session gets is decided by what the provider has free, and the sessions of a task may differ in it; the queue's record names the GPU of each session. A run trained or resumed on another model is not the same to the last bit as one trained on the development's, and no claim of this gate rests on that. The lines and the criteria are the same on every model; the hours and the cost are not.

**At the same time or one after another.** The rule was fixed on 25 November 2026, before any timing. The three runs of a cycle are trained at the same time on one GPU, each a process of its own, only on a GPU model on which a timing has shown that three runs of the first cycle trained together take at most 2.0 times the seconds an epoch of one run trained alone: the median of the three against the one, each in its second epoch, with the seeds 90 to 93. A task that trains them at the same time accepts only such models. On a model without such a timing, and if no timing has been made when the first session starts, they are trained one after another. Where the rule leaves both open, the owner chooses by cost before the first session. Either way each run has its own seed, checkpoints and record, the commands are those of the runbook, and every training comes before the first prediction on the sample; the task file kept with the records shows which it was. A timing after the stamp is made with the development's command for two epochs with the seeds 90 to 93 and before the first session of the gate; its networks are not kept or read. By the timing of 25 November 2026 the A100 does not meet the rule (2.64 against 2.0): on it the runs are trained one after another. For the RTX PRO 6000 and the H100 there is no such timing: one after another there too, unless one is made before the first session.

**Budget**, from the development's records on the RTX PRO 6000: an epoch of the first cycle took 85.55 s in the median and one of the second 107.0 s, so that a seed takes about 9.5 and 4.8 hours. The export of the 160 training and 31 development members took under ten minutes with 24 worker processes, and the labels of the 31 members about five. The evaluation of one run on 31 members took about ten minutes; the sample's evaluation, with 448 pairs for each of three seeds, two readings and the exact value for one of them, is estimated at two to three hours with 24 worker processes. On the A100 runtime of the timing an epoch of the first cycle took 187.4 s, which makes about 21 hours a seed for the first cycle. No epoch of the second cycle was timed there; scaled by the same factor of 2.2 it would take about 10 hours a seed. The runtime's 12 processors leave ten worker processes, so that the data steps and the evaluations are expected to take about two and a half times as long, by the number of processes alone and without a timing: about six to eight hours for the sample's evaluation. A gate run on such a runtime alone would then take about 100 hours in five or six sessions, against about 47 hours in three on the development's runtime. On the author's machine a pair of the eight measured members took about 25 s for the solve at the end rotation and about 54 s for the search of the exact value. These times are estimates and no criterion.

## Procedure

1. Before the stamp: the tests; the host check on a processor; the simulation of the host's task; the independent reading of the code, of this text and of the runbook by readers who wrote none of them and draw nothing of this gate; the timing on the gate's host, if the helper can make it. Then the stamp: the seed is written into `f21/stamp_day.py`, this document is filled in, the configuration and the code are hashed, and the owner commits and pushes. The author reads the commit back from the repository and reports its day beside the seed.
2. On the host, from the stamped code: `write-config` with the configuration hash of this document, which ends with an error unless the code is the stamped code and the host has the configured solver; the host check.
3. The development's members and the further training members are exported; the 31 members of the development are labelled and their labels checked.
4. The sample is exported, labelled and checked.
5. The three seeds are trained, each through both cycles.
6. When every training has ended, each seed predicts the sample. The 31 members of the development and the sample are evaluated. The verdict is written.
7. The records are returned (`records/G-F2.1/`). The author computes the verdict's statistics again from the evaluation's rows with code that shares nothing with `f21/gate.py`, and writes the verdict document.

During the runs the author reads the progress files and the snapshots, which hold what the networks give on the 31 members of the development. No decision is taken on them: the gate runs to its end whatever they show, unless a stop condition applies. Nothing that a network gives on the sample exists before every training has ended, and the readings of the predictions on the sample are not compared with its labels by any other means than the evaluation of step 6.
