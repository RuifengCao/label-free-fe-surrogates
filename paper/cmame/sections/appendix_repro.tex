\subsection{Executable checks}\label{app:repro:checks}

Two further statements, restated from the companion note \cite{CaoSong2026}, bound the
displacement error by the energy gap and describe gradient descent on the energy of one
instance; their checks were pre-registered.

\begin{lemma}[Conditioning]\label{lem:cond}
For every $u\in\R^{n}$,
\begin{equation}
\norm{u-\Ustar}_2 \le \Bigl(\frac{2\gap(u)}{\lambda_{\min}}\Bigr)^{1/2}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac{\norm{u-\Ustar}_2^{2}}{\norm{\Ustar}_2^{2}} \le \kappa\, g(u).
\label{eq:cond}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$2\gap(u)=\norm{e}_K^2\ge\lambda_{\min}\norm{e}_2^2$ gives the first inequality; with
$\norm{\Ustar}_K^2\le\lambda_{\max}\norm{\Ustar}_2^2$ it gives the second.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Modewise contraction]\label{cor:modes}
Let $u^{+}=u-\eta\nabla\Pi_h(u)$ be one gradient-descent step with step size $\eta>0$ on the
energy of one instance. In the eigenbasis of \cref{rem:norms}, the error component in
eigenmode $m$ evolves exactly as
\begin{equation}
\hat e_m^{+} = (1-\eta\lambda_m)\,\hat e_m .
\label{eq:modes}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from $\nabla\Pi_h(u)=K(u-\Ustar)$ (\cref{lem:exact}) and the diagonalisation of
$K$.
\end{proof}

The statements of \cref{sec:theory} and these two are checked numerically, as follows.
\Cref{lem:exact} holds on assembled instances to machine precision. The checks of
\cref{lem:cond}, \cref{cor:modes} and \eqref{eq:cheb} were pre-registered for sixteen
assembled validation instances of each corpus (criteria C5 and KP5 in \cref{tab:criteria});
none was violated. The modewise identity \eqref{eq:modes} held to \numModesPhrase{} on the
extreme eigenvectors; for random perturbations of the solutions, the displacement errors
reached at most \numCondTwo{} and \numCondThree{} of the bound \eqref{eq:cond}, and the
conjugate-gradient ratios at most \numChebTwo{} and \numChebThree{} of the bound
\eqref{eq:cheb}; the condition numbers of these instances are given in
\cref{sec:methods:data}. On the \numAmpRows{} instance-seed evaluations of the readings of
\cref{sec:transfer:amplitude}, the relative energy gap after rescaling, computed from the
energies, agreed with $1-\cos^2\psi$ to within \numAmpIdentity{}. On every validation load
case of the networks measured after the runs, all trained on 1,024 instances (the four
trained networks of the two-dimensional comparison, and the label-free transformer, the
displacement-supervised transformer and the graph network in three dimensions), the energy
was positive exactly when the error's energy norm exceeded the solution's, that is, when the
prediction was further from the solution than the zero field in the energy norm
(\cref{cor:zero}), the rescaling by $\cstar$ never increased the relative energy gap
(\cref{prop:amp}), and the area- or volume-weighted von Mises error obeyed
\eqref{eq:vmboundrel}, with largest ratios of its left side to its right side of
\numSpecBoundMax{} in two dimensions and \numFieldBoundMax{} in three.

The area- or volume-weighted von Mises errors of these networks differed little from the
element-wise ones that the tables report (\cref{sec:theory:stress}). In two dimensions their
medians over the validation load cases were \numSpecVmAreaFree{}, \numSpecVmAreaKnorm{},
\numSpecVmAreaLab{} and \numSpecVmAreaMgn{} for the label-free, the energy-norm-supervised
and the displacement-supervised transformers and the graph network, against element-wise
medians of \numSpecVmElemFree{}, \numSpecVmElemKnorm{}, \numSpecVmElemLab{} and
\numSpecVmElemMgn{}; in three dimensions they were \numFieldVmVolFree{}, \numFieldVmVolLab{}
and \numFieldVmVolMgn{} for the label-free transformer, the displacement-supervised
transformer and the graph network, against element-wise medians of \numFieldVmElemFree{},
\numFieldVmElemLab{} and \numFieldVmElemMgn{}.

\subsection{TF32 rounding}\label{app:repro:tf32}

The networks ran with TF32 matrix products (\cref{sec:methods:training}), whose rounding
varies from node to node and therefore weighs heavily in the energy norm, so each network of
the two-dimensional comparison was also evaluated with TF32 off, after the run. The
rounding, the difference between the two predictions, had a squared energy norm of a median
\numSpecTfFree{} and \numSpecTfKnorm{} of that of the error for the label-free and the
energy-norm-supervised transformers (\numSpecTfLab{} and \numSpecTfMgn{} for the two
networks trained on the displacement error). In the modes with $\lambda_m\ge10^4R(\Ustar)$
it had a median \numSpecTfStiffFree{} and \numSpecTfStiffKnorm{} of the error's squared
energy norm in those modes, on the \numSpecTfStiffN{} triples whose spectra reach that far
(\numSpecTfStiffLoads{} of the \numSpecLoadCases{} pairs of instance and load case, in each
seed). With TF32 off, the median quotients $\rho$ were \numSpecIeeeRqFree{} (label-free),
\numSpecIeeeRqKnorm{} (energy-norm-supervised), \numSpecIeeeRqLab{}
(displacement-supervised) and \numSpecIeeeRqMgn{} (graph network), and the geometric-mean
ratios of $\rho$ and of $g$ between the displacement-supervised and the
energy-norm-supervised transformers rose to \numSpecIeeeLabKnormRq{} and
\numSpecIeeeLabKnormGap{}. The rounding thus places the errors of the two networks trained
in the energy norm higher in the spectrum than they would lie without it, and the readings
as run understate the differences between the networks trained in the two norms.

\subsection{Field plots}\label{app:repro:plots}

The instances shown in the field plots of \cref{sec:checks:ranking} and
Sections~\ref{supp:three}, \ref{supp:two} and~\ref{sec:transfer} were chosen by rules fixed
in code before any field was drawn: in three dimensions, the validation instance with the
largest relative energy gap of the displacement-supervised transformer (the same instance in
all three seeds), the validation instance with the median relative energy gap of the
label-free transformer, and the fine-mesh instance with the median displacement error of the
label-free transformer; in two dimensions, the validation instance with the median relative
energy gap of the displacement-supervised transformer; each in seed 0, the median of $n$
values being the lower one, of rank $\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$ counted from zero in ascending
order. The first three-dimensional rule, and the two-dimensional rule, which was written
after the verdict of the two-dimensional comparison, were chosen after the per-instance
records had been examined, but before any field had been plotted. Each plot shows one load
case of its instance, chosen by the same rule applied to the instance's four load cases (the
largest value, or the second smallest); this was fixed when the plots were made, after the
per-load-case values had been examined but before any field had been drawn. The
three-dimensional plots show the three faces of the box seen in an oblique projection, the
free end on the right; the clamped face and the cavities are hidden.
