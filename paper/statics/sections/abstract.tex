Finite-element surrogates predict the stresses of a new design in one evaluation, but are
usually trained on solutions with a displacement loss and compared by it, although
engineering decisions rest on stresses. Application in engineering: stress analysis of
perforated plates and solids with cavities, parametric families in which holes concentrate
stress. Contribution to artificial intelligence: training and evaluating such surrogates in
the energy norm, with or without solutions, and screening their predictions without
solutions. For linear elastostatics, minimising the assembled discrete potential energy is
exactly supervised regression in this norm, since the energy gap of a prediction is the
strain energy of its stress error, whereas a displacement loss under-weights the stiff modes
that carry the strains. In pre-registered comparisons, changing only the norm of the
supervised loss cut a three-dimensional transformer's median energy-norm error
\numNlThreeNormE{}-fold and its median von Mises error \numNlThreeNormVm{}-fold; in two
dimensions it more than halved the von Mises error, also on held-out instances. In three
dimensions, the transformer trained on the energy alone came within a factor of
\numNlThreeLabelWithin{} of the energy-norm-supervised one in median energy-norm and von
Mises error and beat the displacement-supervised one on all five metrics in every seed.
Given the stiffness matrix, a Ritz projection was more accurate than the label-free
transformer, in the seed means, on every metric but peak stress, and no network transferred
to a substantially finer mesh. The energy ranks predictions of a load case, and its sign
flags those worse than the zero field.
